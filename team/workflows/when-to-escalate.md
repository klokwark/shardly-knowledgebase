# When to escalate

## Escalate to a **manager** when

- You need a permission you do not have: restart, suspend, force delete.
- Money is involved: refund, credit, comped time, a disputed charge.
- A chargeback or legal action is mentioned.
- You have replied twice and it is getting worse.
- You are about to tell an angry customer something you are not certain of.
- You think we should make an exception to a policy.

## Escalate to **engineering** when

- The dashboard or API is wrong and you can reproduce it.
- A provisioning job dead-lettered for a reason that is not an obvious
  one-off.
- Data is inconsistent — status says one thing, behaviour is another.
- **Three customers report the same thing in a short window.** Two is a
  coincidence.
- Something in the platform is slow in a way that is new.

## Escalate to **security** immediately when

- An account may be compromised.
- Somebody reports a vulnerability.
- You see evidence of data reaching the wrong customer.
- A staff account is behaving oddly.

Security does not wait for the normal path. Flag it in the staff channel now,
then write the note.

## How to escalate

**1. Establish what you can first.** Not "it is broken" — what you tried, what
you saw, what you ruled out.

**2. Internal note** on the ticket with all of it.

**3. Flag it** in the staff channel with the ticket number.

**4. Tell the customer** you have, and when you will come back.

**5. Keep it.** It is still your ticket. Chase it.

## What escalation is not

- A way to stop thinking about a hard ticket.
- A way to avoid saying no. If the answer is no and you know it is no, say it.
- Reassigning and walking away. Without your note the next person starts from
  nothing, and the customer explains it twice.

## If you are not sure

Ask in the staff channel. "Is this worth escalating?" costs one message.
Guessing wrong in either direction costs more — an unescalated outage, or an
engineer pulled off work for a typo in someone's config.
