# Writing a reply

## Before you type

- Read the whole ticket. All of it, including notes.
- Look at their account. Plan, servers, status, recent activity.
- Check [what we have not built](../reference/not-built-yet.md) if the answer
  touches domains, uptime, alerts or teams.
- Decide what the actual question is. It is often not the one in the subject.

## The structure

**1. Answer.** First sentence.

**2. Steps.** Numbered if there is more than one. Exact names of exact
buttons — "Bot settings → Variables", not "the settings area".

**3. Why**, if it stops it happening again.

**4. What next**, if anything is open.

## Ask for what you need — once, at the start

The single biggest cause of a three-day ticket is three round trips for
information you could have asked for in the first reply.

For anything technical, ask for:

- **The last 50 lines of the console log.** Always.
- What they changed before it started.
- Which server, if they have several.

Ask for all of it at once. Three questions in one message beats one question
three times.

## Link, do not paste

Link the knowledge base article. It stays current, it has the screenshots, and
they can find it again.

Paste the key sentence *and* link it, if it is one line.

If there is no article and there should be, write one. Ten minutes now, saved
every time after.

## Things not to write

- **"As I mentioned in my previous message"** — passive-aggressive and they
  did not read it, which is a signal your message was too long.
- **"That's not possible"** when you mean "we have not built it". Very
  different things.
- **"It works on my end"** — true and useless.
- **A wall of text.** If it is over six lines, cut it or use a list.
- **Anything you are not sure is true.** Check or say you will check.

## Correcting yourself

If you were wrong, say so plainly and move on.

> I was wrong about that — backups are two slots on every plan, including
> Starter. Sorry for the confusion.

Once. No agonising.

## Before you send

Read it back as the customer. Does it answer the question? Does it tell them
what to do? Would you be satisfied?

Then check nothing in it promises something we do not have.
