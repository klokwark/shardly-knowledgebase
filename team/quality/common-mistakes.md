# Common mistakes

The ones we actually make, in rough order of how often.

## Answering without the log

You guess, they try it, it does not work, you have lost a day. **Ask for the
last 50 lines in your first reply**, every time, on anything technical.

## Confusing plan status with process state

`deployment.status` describes the server's life. The console says whether the
process is running. A server that is `active` with a stopped bot is normal.

Saying "your server is running fine" when their bot is down is technically
true and completely useless.

## Saying "deleted" when you mean "suspended"

Suspended means the files are all still there. Deleted means they are gone.

Getting this wrong causes genuine panic. Check which it is before you type
either word.

## Repeating a marketing claim

Subdomains, crash DMs, measured uptime, automatic resharding. The site says
them, we do not have them. Read
[not built yet](../reference/not-built-yet.md).

## Promising a refund

Support and managers cannot issue refunds. "I will get you an answer today" is
a promise you can keep. "I will get you a refund" might not be yours to make.

## Using `waiting` to tidy the queue

The clock stops on `waiting`. If the ball is with you and you park it there,
you have hidden a ticket rather than handled it.

## Escalating without establishing anything

"It is broken, over to you" wastes an engineer's time. Say what you tried,
what you saw, what you ruled out — and **what you actually need**.

## Going quiet

The most common complaint is not slowness, it is not knowing. "Still working
on it, will update tomorrow" costs a minute.

## Closing with an open question

Read the whole message before closing. People bury a second question in the
last line.

## Over-apologising

Three apologies read as defensive and make a small problem feel enormous. Once
if we were wrong, then get on with it.

## Matching their energy

Never. A calm, specific reply to a furious message defuses it. A defensive one
guarantees a second round.

## Not writing the article

If two people asked this month, write it. Ten minutes now, saved every time
after — and the third person never opens the ticket at all.
