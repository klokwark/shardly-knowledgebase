# Quality and review

## Reviewing each other

Not a performance exercise. It is how the handbook stays true and how new
people get better quickly.

**Weekly, informally:** pick three closed tickets each and read somebody
else's. Say one thing that worked and one that could have been shorter or
clearer.

**Always review:** anything that went badly, anything escalated, and the first
ten a new agent sends.

## What good looks like

- Answer in the first sentence
- Exact names, exact numbers
- Asked for everything needed at once
- Linked the article
- Honest about what we cannot do
- Ends with what happens next

## What to flag

- A claim we cannot deliver
- A number that is wrong
- A refund or credit promised by somebody who cannot issue one
- Credentials quoted in a ticket
- A `waiting` that should have been `in_progress`
- Anything written about a customer that would be embarrassing if they read it

Flag the ticket, not the person, and say it to them directly before saying it
to anybody else.

## Ratings

Tickets can be rated by the customer. Read them, and treat them as weak
evidence.

A low rating on a ticket where you said no correctly is not a failure. A high
rating on a ticket where you promised something we cannot do is not a success.
Rate the reply, not the reaction.

## Signals worth acting on

**The same question three times in a month** → missing article, or missing
deflection entry.

**A category growing** → look at why. More `billing` tickets often means the
billing copy is unclear, not that more people have billing problems.

**Tickets taking more round trips than they used to** → usually we have
stopped asking for the log up front.

**A rise in "not built yet" conversations** → the site is drifting further
ahead of the product, and that is worth raising loudly.

## Monthly

Half an hour, together:

- Worst ticket of the month, and what would have prevented it
- What we wrote down that turned out to be wrong
- Which article is missing
- What the queue is telling us about the product

The last one matters most. **Support is the first place a real problem becomes
visible**, and a queue nobody reads for patterns is an early warning system
switched off.
