# Playbook: they want something we have not built

## Read this first

[What we have not built](../reference/not-built-yet.md). All of it, before you
answer anything about domains, uptime, crash alerts, teams or SSO.

## The trap

**Some of these are on our marketing site.** A customer reading the landing
page has been told their bot gets `bot-name.shardly.xyz` with a certificate,
and that they get a DM the moment it crashes.

Neither is built.

So the customer is not confused — **they were told**. If you reply as though
they misunderstood, you compound it. And if you repeat the claim, you turn a
disappointment into being lied to, which is much harder to recover from.

## What to say

Be direct, take the hit, give them something real.

> You are right that the site says that, and I am sorry — it is not built yet.
> The subdomain feature is on our list but there is no date I can give you
> honestly today. I am flagging the site copy internally, because you should
> not have found out this way.
>
> In the meantime, what people do is [alternative]. If the subdomain is a
> blocker for what you are building, tell me and I will make sure it is
> weighted properly — it genuinely does affect what gets built next.

## Do not

- **"Coming soon."** Meaningless, and they have heard it.
- **Invent a date.** "Next quarter" from support becomes a promise.
- **Pretend it exists** and hope they do not check.
- **Blame marketing.** Never blame a colleague to a customer.

## If they bought because of it

That is a refund conversation. Escalate to admin with a note saying they
bought on the strength of a claim we do not deliver.

We should refund those. Cheerfully.

## Log it

Every time this happens, note it and raise it in the staff channel. Two
purposes:

1. Feature demand is real data about what to build next.
2. **Site copy that promises something we do not have is a bug**, and the
   queue is where it becomes visible.

## For genuine feature requests

Different, and nicer. Thank them, be honest about whether it is likely, never
promise.

> Not something we do today, and I will not pretend it is imminent. I have
> noted it — we do read these when deciding what to build, and knowing the use
> case helps more than the request on its own. What would you be using it for?
