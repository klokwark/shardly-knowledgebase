# Playbook: refund request

**Category:** `billing` · **Escalate to admin.** Support and managers cannot
issue refunds.

## Your job

Gather the facts, form a view, hand it over. Do not promise or refuse a refund
yourself.

## Establish

- **What did they buy, and when?**
- **What went wrong?** Our fault, their misunderstanding, or a change of mind?
- **Did they use it?** A server that ran for three weeks is different from one
  that never built.
- **Have they asked before?**

## Where it usually lands

**Refund, without argument:**

- We could not deliver. Paid and never built, or a fault on our side made it
  unusable for a meaningful part of the period.
- Double purchase, or the wrong tier, reported quickly.
- Charged for a sold-out tier.

**Usually not:**

- Changed their mind mid-period.
- Their code did not work. The server did what it was paid for.
- Forgot to cancel — though we are human about it the first time, and often
  should be.

## What to say while you escalate

> Let me put this in front of the person who can action it. Here is what I
> have: you bought Pro on the 3rd, it never finished building, and you have
> not been able to use it. That looks like our problem rather than yours. I
> will come back to you today.

**Do not say "I will get you a refund."** You do not know yet. "I will get you
an answer today" is a promise you can keep.

## Chargebacks

If one is mentioned or already filed: **escalate to admin immediately** and do
not argue about it in the ticket.

> I would much rather sort this directly — a chargeback takes weeks and
> freezes the account including the servers you are still using. Let me get
> this in front of someone today.

A chargeback costs us a fee and takes weeks. Almost every one we have seen
would have been settled faster by a ticket, so it is worth one honest attempt
to redirect.

## Write the note

Amount, date, invoice number, what went wrong, and your recommendation. Admin
should be able to decide without re-reading the thread.
