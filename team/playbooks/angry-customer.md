# Playbook: angry customer

## First

**Tone is not priority.** A furious message about an invoice is a billing
ticket. Do not promote it because somebody shouted — that teaches people to
shout and pushes down a genuinely urgent ticket from someone polite.

Work it at its real priority, and answer it well.

## They are usually right about something

Even when the anger is out of proportion, there is generally a real problem
underneath: lost work, a bill they did not expect, a third message with no
reply.

**Find that, and address it.** Responding to the tone instead of the substance
is how a bad ticket becomes a bad week.

## The shape of the reply

1. **Name what went wrong**, without defending it.
2. **Apologise once**, if we got it wrong. Once.
3. **Say what you are doing about it**, concretely.
4. **Say when they will hear back.**

> You are right, and I am sorry — you asked on Monday and nobody replied until
> now. That is on us.
>
> On the actual problem: your server was suspended because the renewal failed
> on the 3rd. Your files are all still there. Update the card under Billing →
> Manage billing and it comes straight back, or tell me and I will hold it
> while you sort the bank out.

## Do not

- **Match their energy.** Ever.
- **Explain our internal reasons.** "We were short-staffed" is our problem,
  not theirs.
- **Over-apologise.** Three apologies read as defensive and make it feel
  worse.
- **Go silent.** The thing that makes people angriest is not slowness — it is
  not knowing.
- **Argue about the tone.** Even when it is unfair.

## When it is abusive

Rudeness is fine. Abuse is not.

Once, calmly:

> I want to get this sorted for you and I am going to keep working on it. I
> would ask you to keep it civil — I am a person and I am on your side here.

If it continues, hand it to a manager. Nobody on this team has to absorb
abuse, and there is no prize for enduring it silently.

## When we were wrong

Say so plainly. Do not hedge, do not distribute the blame, do not explain the
process that caused it.

> We got this wrong. Your server should never have been deleted and I am
> sorry.

Then fix what can be fixed, and be honest about what cannot. Customers forgive
mistakes far more readily than they forgive evasiveness.

## Afterwards

If it went badly, say so in a note and mention it to a manager. Not to
apportion blame — to find the thing that produced it. Most furious tickets
start as an ordinary one that nobody answered.
