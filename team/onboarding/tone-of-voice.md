# Tone of voice

How we write. The product's voice and support's voice are the same voice.

## The shape of a good reply

1. **The answer**, in the first sentence.
2. **What to do**, as steps if there is more than one.
3. **Why**, briefly, if it helps them next time.
4. **What happens next**, if anything is outstanding.

Not: an apology, a paragraph of sympathy, then the answer at the bottom.

## Rules

**Answer first.** People are scanning. Put the conclusion where they are
looking.

**Be specific.** "Two backup slots on every plan" not "backups are available".
"Within a minute" not "shortly". If you do not know the number, find it or
leave it out — never invent a plausible one.

**Say what to do.** "Open Bot settings and set DISCORD_TOKEN" not "you may
wish to review your configuration".

**Short sentences.** Full stops are free.

**No hedging.** "It does not do that yet" beats "unfortunately that
functionality is not currently available at this time".

**Never blame them.** Not "you uploaded it wrong" but "the files need to be at
the top level — they are currently inside a folder".

**Never blame a colleague in writing.** Not "whoever told you that was
wrong". "Let me correct that" is enough, and the ticket is a record.

## Words we do not use

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Unfortunately | (nothing — just say it) |
| We apologise for any inconvenience | Sorry about that — or nothing |
| Please be advised | (nothing) |
| As per our policy | Because… |
| Kindly | Please |
| Reach out | Ask, write, open a ticket |
| Utilise | Use |
| At this time | Yet, today, or nothing |
| Ticket #123 has been escalated | I have passed this to… |

## Apologising

Once, early, and only when we got it wrong.

- We broke it → "Sorry — that was our fault." Then the fix.
- It broke and it is not our fault → do not apologise. Help.
- They are angry and we were right → "I understand why that is frustrating."
  Not sorry.

Repeated apologies read as defensive and make a small problem feel large.

## Saying no

Say it, say why in one line, and say what they can do instead.

> We do not support custom domains yet — it is on the list but not built. The
> free subdomain is not built either, so today your bot has no public URL. If
> you need one, running a small service elsewhere and pointing it at your bot
> is what people do in the meantime.

Not "I'm afraid that's not something we're able to offer at this time."

## Length

As long as it needs to be, and not a sentence longer. A three-line answer to a
three-line question is respectful, not curt.

## Names

Use theirs if you know it. Sign with yours. You are a person, not "the Shardly
Support Team".
