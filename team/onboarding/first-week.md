# Your first week

## Day one

**Read.** All of `onboarding/`, then `workflows/working-the-queue.md` and
`workflows/triage-and-priority.md`. Skim the customer articles so you know
what exists to link to.

**Get access.** Your account needs a role — ask an admin. Confirm you can see
`/app/staff` and that the queue loads.

**Set up your own bot.** Genuinely. Buy or get comped a Starter, deploy a
Discord.js bot, break it on purpose, fix it. You cannot support a product you
have not used, and half the queue is problems you will have had yourself by
Friday.

## Day two and three

**Shadow.** Read closed tickets, especially ones that went badly. Read the
whole thread, including internal notes.

**Draft, do not send.** Pick open tickets, write the reply you would send,
have somebody read it. Five drafts reviewed beats fifty sent badly.

## Day four and five

**Send, with review.** Take real tickets in the `account` and `billing`
categories first — they are the most scripted. Have someone read your first
ten before they go.

**Learn the escalation line.** By the end of the week you should know what you
handle, what goes to a manager, and what goes to engineering. See
`workflows/when-to-escalate.md`.

## End of week one, you should be able to

- Explain the difference between a plan status and a process state without
  looking it up.
- Say what happens to files when a subscription lapses.
- Name three things the site claims that we have not built.
- Find a customer's activity log and say what it does and does not show.
- Answer "my bot will not start" without asking anybody.

## What nobody expects of you

To know the answer. This is a technical product and the queue is technical.
Asking a colleague costs a minute; guessing at a customer costs their trust
and somebody else's afternoon.

## The one habit worth building now

**Ask for the log in your first reply**, every time, when it is a technical
ticket. The single biggest cause of a three-day ticket is three round trips to
get information that could have been asked for at the start.
