# Greetings and closings

## Opening

**Ordinary**

> Hi {name} — thanks for writing in.

**They have waited too long**

> Hi {name}. Sorry for the slow reply — you should have heard from us sooner.

Only when it is true. An apology for a two-hour reply devalues the real one.

**They are upset**

> Hi {name}. I have read the whole thread and I can see why this is
> frustrating. Let me sort it out.

No apology yet — that comes when you know whose fault it was.

## Acknowledging when you do not have the answer

> Thanks {name} — I have got this and I am looking into it now. I will come
> back to you {timeframe} either way, even if the answer is that we are still
> digging.

Give a real timeframe and keep it. This one is the difference between a
patient customer and an angry one.

## Closing

**Solved**

> That should be it — {what you did}. If it comes back, just reply here and
> this ticket reopens.
>
> {your name}

**Solved, their end**

> Glad that sorted it. Anything else, reply here.
>
> {your name}

**Waiting on them**

> Let me know how that goes and I will pick it up from there. No rush — this
> ticket stays open.
>
> {your name}

**No, with an alternative**

> Not something we can do today, I am afraid — {one line of why}. What does
> work is {alternative}. Happy to help you set that up if it is useful.
>
> {your name}

## Never

- "Please let us know if you have any further questions." Say it like a
  person.
- "Your ticket has been resolved." They decide that.
- "Best regards, The Shardly Support Team." You have a name.
