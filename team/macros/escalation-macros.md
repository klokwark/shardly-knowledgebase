# Escalation macros

## Telling the customer you have escalated

> I have passed this to our engineers with everything you have sent.
>
> I will update you by {specific time} either way — including if the answer at
> that point is that we are still looking.

Always a real time. Never "as soon as possible".

## Waiting on engineering, no news

> Quick update: no answer yet, but this has not been forgotten — {name} is
> looking at it. I will come back to you {timeframe}.

Send this rather than going quiet. Silence is the single most common thing
customers complain about, well ahead of slowness.

## Needs a permission you do not have

> I need a colleague to action this one — restarting a server is above my
> level, deliberately, since a misplaced click there is somebody's bot going
> offline.
>
> I have asked and it should be done within {timeframe}. I will confirm here.

Honest, and it tells them the delay is a safeguard rather than bureaucracy.

## Handing over at end of shift

> I am finishing for the day, so {name} is picking this up — they have the
> full history and everything I have tried.
>
> You will not need to explain it again.

Then write the internal note that makes that true.

## The internal note that goes with it

Not sent to the customer. Structure:

```
ESCALATING — {what kind}

What they reported:
  {one line}

What I have established:
  - {fact}
  - {fact}

Ruled out:
  - {thing}, because {evidence}

What I need:
  {specific ask}

Told the customer:
  Update by {time}.
```

The last two lines matter most. An escalation without a specific ask becomes
somebody else's puzzle, and one without a promised time becomes a ticket
nobody chases.

## Chargeback mentioned

> I would much rather sort this directly. A chargeback takes weeks and freezes
> the account in the meantime — including servers you are still using.
>
> Let me get this in front of someone who can action it today. What is the
> outcome you want?

Then escalate to admin immediately. Do not argue the merits in the ticket.
